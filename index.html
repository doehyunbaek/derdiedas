          01 · to be

          sein

          Sein connects a subject with an identity, quality, place, or condition. It also forms the perfect tense of verbs that describe movement or a change of state.

          identity or state · sein + complement
          perfect · sein + past participle

          Sie ist Ärztin. · Er ist nach Hause gegangen.She is a doctor. · He has gone home.

          02 · to have

          haben

          Haben expresses possession and relationships. As an auxiliary, it forms the perfect tense for most German verbs.

          possession · haben + object
          perfect · haben + past participle

          Ich habe Zeit. · Wir haben lange gewartet.I have time. · We waited for a long time.

          03 · to become / will

          werden

          Werden describes becoming or change. It also builds the future and, with a past participle, the passive voice—one reason it is particularly frequent in formal writing.

          change · werden + adjective/noun
          future · werden + infinitive
          passive · werden + past participle

          Es wird kalt. · Das Haus wird gebaut.It is getting cold. · The house is being built.

          04 · can / to be able to

          können

          Können expresses ability, possibility, or permission. It often corresponds to English can or could.

          können + infinitive at the end of the clause

          Ich kann morgen kommen.I can come tomorrow.

          05 · must / to have to

          müssen

          Müssen marks necessity or strong obligation. Its negative deserves care: nicht müssen means “not have to,” rather than “must not.”

          müssen + infinitive at the end of the clause

          Du musst gehen. · Du musst nicht gehen.You must leave. · You do not have to leave.

          06 · to want to

          wollen

          Wollen states an intention, desire, or plan. It is direct and can sound forceful; requests are often softened with möchten.

          wollen + infinitive at the end of the clause

          Wir wollen Deutsch lernen.We want to learn German.

          07 · should / to be supposed to

          sollen

          Sollen conveys an instruction, expectation, recommendation, or claim originating with someone else. It often answers: “According to whom?”

          sollen + infinitive at the end of the clause

          Ich soll den Arzt anrufen. · Er soll reich sein.I am supposed to call the doctor. · He is said to be rich.

          08 · may / to be allowed to

          dürfen

          Dürfen concerns permission. Unlike nicht müssen, nicht dürfen is a prohibition: something is not allowed.

          dürfen + infinitive at the end of the clause

          Darf ich hereinkommen? · Hier darf man nicht rauchen.May I come in? · Smoking is not allowed here.

          09 · to like / may

          mögen

          Mögen usually expresses liking when followed by a noun. Its subjunctive form möchten makes a polite wish; in formal language, mag can also signal possibility.

          liking · mögen + object
          polite wish · möchten + infinitive/object

          Ich mag Kaffee. · Ich möchte bezahlen.I like coffee. · I would like to pay.
